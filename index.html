01

The first 48 hours

The police have been. Now the clock is ticking – CCTV and doorbell footage is often overwritten within days, sometimes hours.

Get the paperwork

Write down the crime/incident reference number, the attending officer's name, collar number and station, and how to add evidence later. Your insurer will ask for it on the very first call.

Hunt the footage – today

Knock on doors along the street. Ask about Ring/Nest doorbells, home CCTV, shop and pub cameras, buses and taxis. Ask neighbours to save the clip now and send it to you or upload it to your police force.

Dashcams

Post a calm appeal in local Facebook/Nextdoor groups for dashcam footage of the road at the time. Don't name or accuse anyone publicly.

Photograph everything

Wide shots of the scene, every panel of damage, skid marks, debris, the road position and nearby cameras. Turn on timestamps. Back them up to the cloud.

Keep the debris

Bag up any broken lights, trim, mirror glass or bits with part numbers – and photograph paint transfer (a different colour on your silver paint is gold dust). Tell the police you have it.

Witnesses

Names, phone numbers and a short note of what each person saw, while it's fresh. Pass them to the police and the insurer.
"Footage gets wiped. Memories fade. Move fast in the first 48 hours."

What the police will – and won't – do

Failing to stop after a collision and failing to report it are offences under the Road Traffic Act 1988 (s.170). The police will log the crime and may follow up leads such as a registration, ANPR or footage – but with damage-only incidents and no suspect details, investigations are often closed quickly. They won't chase your insurance or pay for the damage. Your strongest move is to hand them good evidence. You can add new information online via your force's site or police.uk; see also the gov.uk guidance on reporting a collision. If a car is spotted with matching damage, report it – don't confront anyone.

Sources: Road Traffic Act 1988 s.170 · police.uk

02

Don't get stung by the insurer

You did nothing wrong. Your job now is to make sure every record says so.

Call them promptly

Most policies require you to report incidents "as soon as reasonably possible". Say clearly: non-fault, hit-and-run, other driver untraced, reported to police, and give the crime reference.

The no-claims question

When the other driver is never found, your insurer usually can't recover its costs from anyone – so by default many treat it as a claim that can affect your no-claims discount (NCD), even though you weren't to blame. But many comprehensive policies include an "uninsured driver promise" – and some extend it to untraced/hit-and-run drivers – that protects your NCD and refunds your excess. Read the wording carefully: some promises only apply if you can give the other vehicle's registration. Protected NCD (if you paid for it) also helps.

Check your policy for: "uninsured driver promise" · "untraced driver" · "hit and run" · "protected no claims discount" · "courtesy car" · "excess". Ask the insurer to confirm in writing how NCD and excess will be treated.

If he's only third-party: the MIB

The Motor Insurers' Bureau compensates victims of untraced drivers under the 2017 Untraced Drivers' Agreement. The hard truth: for an untraced vehicle, MIB generally only pays for vehicle damage if someone also suffered "significant personal injury" in the same incident (death, 2+ nights in hospital or 3+ outpatient sessions), and then with a £400 excess. For a damage-only hit-and-run, a third-party-only driver is unlikely to recover the car's value from MIB – which is why comprehensive cover matters. If the driver is later identified but turns out uninsured, a different MIB scheme applies. Time limits: 3 years for injury; 6 years for property damage in England & Wales (5 in Scotland) – but apply early.

Sources: MIB eligibility · mib.org.uk

Telling future insurers – truthfully

When asked about accidents or claims (usually the last 5 years), declare it, accurately, as a non-fault hit-and-run by an untraced driver. Insurers share claims data (e.g. via CUE), so non-disclosure can be spotted – and under the Consumer Insurance (Disclosure and Representations) Act 2012 a careless misrepresentation can let an insurer reduce a claim or cancel cover. Stating "non-fault" correctly is what keeps the premium hit to a minimum.

Shop around

Since the FCA's 2022 pricing rules, insurers can't charge renewing customers more than a new customer for the same cover – but shopping around still finds better deals. Use at least two comparison sites (they don't all list the same insurers) and check direct-only insurers too. For a classic or second car, try specialist and agreed-value insurers.

Treated unfairly?

Complain to the insurer first, in writing. If you're unhappy with the final response, or after 8 weeks, you can go to the free Financial Ombudsman Service (usually within 6 months of the final response). Citizens Advice can help too.

Sources: FCA pricing practices · FOS: how to complain · ABI · CIDRA 2012

03

The write-off cheque

Older cars get written off easily because repairs cost more than they're worth. Don't accept the first number without a fight.

CategoryMeaningBack on the road?
Cat AScrap entirelyNo
Cat BBody crushed, parts can be salvagedNo
Cat SStructural damage (chassis/crumple zones)Yes, once professionally repaired – must be re-registered with DVLA
Cat NNon-structural damage onlyYes, after repair – no DVLA re-registration needed

Source: gov.uk – if your vehicle is written off

How the offer works

You're paid the market value just before the incident – what it would have cost to buy an equivalent car (same model, age, mileage, condition) – minus your excess. Insurers use trade guides, which often undervalue cherished older roadsters.

"Trade guides don't know your MX-5's history. You have to show them."

Evidence checklist to challenge a low offer

  • 5+ adverts for 2003 NB-FL (Mk2.5) MX-5s with similar engine, mileage and spec – screenshots with prices and dates (AutoTrader, eBay, PistonHeads, MX-5 Owners Club classifieds)
  • Service history, receipts for recent work (cambelt, tyres, rust repairs, sills, new hood)
  • Recent MOT certificate and clean MOT history
  • Photos of the car before the incident showing condition
  • Receipts for modifications and accessories (if declared to the insurer)
  • A polite written rejection of the offer, explaining your valuation and attaching evidence

Keep it or let it go?

For Cat S/N you can usually retain the car: the insurer deducts its salvage value from the payout. Good if it's repairable cheaply or valuable for parts – but expect higher insurance and lower resale. Before it goes: remove personal items, the spare key, any non-standard parts you're allowed to keep (ask first), and clear the dashcam/SD card.

DVLA, V5C and tax

  • Follow the insurer's instructions on the V5C (logbook) – usually you send them the V5C or the relevant part
  • Your vehicle tax is cancelled when DVLA is told it's written off – a refund for full remaining months is sent automatically
  • Transfer or retain a private plate before the car is handed over, if you have one
  • Cancel the policy on the car once settled; you may be able to transfer cover to the replacement

Courtesy car or hire

Check the policy: many courtesy cars are only provided while your car is being repaired at an approved garage – not when it's a write-off. Some policies or add-ons (guaranteed hire car) cover total losses. Credit-hire firms are mainly for non-fault claims against an identified driver, so be cautious if offered one here.

04

Come back better

Your payout plus any savings sets the budget. Here's how to turn bad luck into a better drive.

Mazda MX-5 NB/NB-FL

The devil you know. Hunt for rust-free sills and a good hood. Owners' club cars are often cherished.

Mazda MX-5 NC (2005–15)

Step up: stiffer, roomier, the hard-top RC folds electrically. Often within reach at £4–8k.

Toyota MR2 Roadster

Mid-engined, reliable, cheap to insure. Check for oil use on early cars.

BMW Z4 (E85) / Honda S2000

More power and more presence – budget for higher insurance and running costs.

Buying checks – never skip

  • History check (HPI or similar): outstanding finance, stolen, previous write-off category
  • Free MOT history on gov.uk – look for rust advisories on sills, subframes and chassis rails
  • Check vehicle details with DVLA match the V5C
  • V5C in the seller's name and address; VIN matches the car
  • Inspect in daylight, test-drive, check the hood for leaks
Quote before you buy. Get insurance quotes on the exact model and engine before handing over money – and check its insurance group. Your hit-and-run (declared as non-fault) will feature in quotes, so compare.

What to say to the insurer

You: "Hi, I need to report a claim. My car was hit while parked [or: while driving] on [date] at about [time] on [street, town]. The other driver left the scene without stopping and hasn't been identified."

You: "It's a non-fault hit-and-run. The police attended and the crime reference is [number], officer [name/collar number]."

You: "I have photos, [witness details / possible footage / debris]. Where should I send them?"

Ask: "Does my policy have an uninsured or untraced driver promise? How will this affect my no-claims discount and my excess? Can you confirm that in writing?"

Ask: "Will it be recorded as non-fault? Am I entitled to a courtesy or hire car if it's written off?"

Ask: "If it's a total loss, how will you value it, and can I see the valuation evidence?"

Finish: "Can I have the claim number and the name of the handler, please?" – then note the date and time of the call.

The one-page checklist

Print this checklist
  • Crime reference number + officer name/collar number noted
  • Door-knocked the street for doorbell/CCTV footage (within 24–48h)
  • Local appeal posted for dashcam footage
  • All damage and scene photographed, backed up
  • Debris and paint transfer bagged and photographed
  • Witness names and numbers collected
  • Insurer called: "non-fault, untraced hit-and-run", claim number noted
  • Policy checked for untraced/uninsured driver promise, NCD and excess
  • Asked for NCD and excess treatment in writing
  • MIB considered if third-party only or injured
  • Comparable adverts saved for valuation challenge
  • Personal items, spare key and allowed mods removed
  • Private plate retained (if any); V5C steps followed
  • Tax refund confirmed
  • Replacement shortlisted, history + MOT checked, insurance quoted first
  • Complaint to insurer → Financial Ombudsman if treated unfairly
